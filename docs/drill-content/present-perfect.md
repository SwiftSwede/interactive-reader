# Drill content — present_perfect (grammar family, tag: `present_perfect`)

Display name: **Present Perfect** · Levels: P (pre-int) + I (int) · Prereqs: present_continuous (per seeds)

## Teach card

**HOOK (Kyle only — write this):** the honest rule. What it should convey (my suggested angle — your call): Spanish uses "hoy he desayunado" where English says "I had breakfast today" — for **finished moments in the past, English almost always uses past simple**. Present perfect is for two jobs only: *experience up to now* ("I have tried ceviche") and *situations that continue into now* ("I have lived here for years"). The check-answer L1 rule 7 says exactly this; your hook should make students distrust the Spanish map.

## Word bank (proposed — Kyle ratifies)

Signal words that legitimately invite present perfect: never · ever · already · yet · just · so far.
NOTE: **for/since excluded entirely** — that's the `for_vs_since` family; mixing it here would break the one-target rule.

## Cloze items (typed; MCQ only on first round)

1. **P** — I ___ never tried ceviche. → **have** (MCQ: have / had / did)
2. **P** — She ___ already finished her homework. → **has** (MCQ: has / have / is)
3. **P** — We ___ just arrived at the beach. → **have** (MCQ: have / did / are)
4. **I** — My parents ___ visited Mexico City twice. → **have** (MCQ: have / has / did)
5. **I** — He ___ not decided yet. → **has** (MCQ: has / have / does)
6. **I** — ___ you ever worked from home? → **Have** (MCQ: Have / Did / Are)

## Translation items (ES → EN) — the real test of rule 7

The pre-int trap: Spanish present perfect for TODAY's finished actions.

1. **P** — Hoy he desayunado arepas. → *I had arepas for breakfast today.* (KEY ITEM — the trap answer "I have had arepas for breakfast today" is exactly what the Spanish map produces; the correct English is past simple)
2. **P** — Nunca he probado el ceviche. → *I have never tried ceviche.*
3. **I** — Esta mañana le he escrito a mi hermana. → *I wrote to my sister this morning.* (again: past simple, not present perfect)
4. **I** — ¿Has trabajado desde casa alguna vez? → *Have you ever worked from home?*

## What I deliberately avoided

- **No for/since anywhere** (that's the `for_vs_since` family).
- No present perfect continuous (separate tag, prerequisites include this one).
- Items 1 and 3 of translations are the pedagogical heart: Spanish perfect → English past simple with today/this-morning time markers. If you'd rather teach the *form* before the *usage contrast*, tell me and I'll split the set: form-items first, contrast-items after the first recall.
- One item pair is deliberately near-duplicate (experience question in cloze #6 + translation #4) — spaced repetition likes different formats hitting the same pattern; cut one if it feels repetitive.
