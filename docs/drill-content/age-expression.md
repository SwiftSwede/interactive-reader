# Drill content — age_expression (error family, tag: `age_expression`)

Display name: **Age — I am 25** · Levels: P (pre-int) + I (int)

## Teach card

**HOOK (Kyle only — write this):** the honest rule. What it should convey: Spanish *tener* años → English **BE**, not have. "Tengo 25 años" is the single most literal Spanish-to-English transfer your students make. One line, no exceptions to list: age is always *I am / she is / they are* + number. Your voice.

## Word bank (proposed — Kyle ratifies)

The trap family, as it actually shows up:
- **I have 25 years** → *I am 25 (years old)*
- **How many years do you have?** → *How old are you?*
- **My sister has 19 years** → *My sister is 19.*
- **We have the same age** → *We are the same age.* (I'd cut this one — rarer; your call)

## Cloze items (typed; MCQ only on first round)

1. **P** — My brother ___ 14. → **is** (MCQ: is / has / has got — "has" is the honest trap)
2. **P** — I ___ 32 years old. → **am** (MCQ: am / have / be)
3. **P** — My grandma is 80. She ___ very active. → **is** (MCQ: is / has / feels — keep; reinforces BE for states, no age collision... cut if you want strict one-train-per-session)
4. **I** — His kids ___ 7 and 9. → **are** (MCQ: are / have / is)
5. **I** — ¿Cuántos años tienes? Choose the correct English. → **How old are you?** (MCQ: How old are you? / How many years do you have? / What age do you have? — every distractor is a real Spanish-map transfer a student actually produces. The earlier "___ are you?" + "How many years" stem built the implausible hybrid "How many years are you?", which nobody says — bad distractor, fixed.)

## Translation items (ES → EN)

1. **P** — Tengo 25 años. → *I am 25 years old.* (also accept: *I am 25.*)
2. **P** — ¿Cuántos años tienes? → *How old are you?*
3. **I** — Mi mamá tiene 60 años y todavía trabaja. → *My mom is 60 (years old) and she still works.*
4. **I** — Mis primos tienen la misma edad que yo. → *My cousins are the same age as me.*

## What I deliberately avoided

- No item sneaks in another trap (no "I am agree," no prepositions) — age is the only target.
- "How old" item tests the question form because students produce "How many years do you have?" verbatim in class — flag it if you'd rather drill only statements first.
- Item 3 is borderline (two BE-verbs, only one age-related): cut it if you want zero dilution.
